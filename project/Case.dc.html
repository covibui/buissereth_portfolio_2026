A wellness clinic, rebuilt around one system

The situation

VMC's brand and operations had grown faster than its systems. Visual assets were inconsistent across print and digital — a logo treated one way on a flyer, another way on Instagram, another on the front desk signage. None of it was wrong, exactly. It just never agreed with itself.

Underneath the visual drift was an operational one. The workflows that ran the clinic — how a promotion went out, how a new hire learned the brand, how an asset got made — lived in people's heads. That works until you want to grow, and then every new person is a retraining cost and every campaign is a negotiation.

"The brand wasn't broken. It just lived in too many heads — and you can't scale what only exists in people."

The reframe

I treated this as a systems problem, not a logo problem. I audited the existing brand and operations together, then built a single source of truth: a design system covering both digital and print, paired with documented, repeatable workflows for the team.

Every decision optimized for consistency and low maintenance — the test was always "can the clinic run this without a designer on call?" Templates over bespoke files, rules over taste, documentation over tribal knowledge.

What became possible

The clinic now runs on a unified brand system and a set of operational templates the team uses daily. Asset production stopped being a one-off scramble, brand consistency became the default rather than the exception, and onboarding a new hire turned into a process instead of an apprenticeship.
